# Spiders dropping on a dragline and climbing back (for v2.0124)

Asked by J's L1 note ("better dropping animations, and bending back up to attach to a surface"). A quick look at
published spider behaviour, to ground the R2 brute's drops:

- **Head first, on the dragline.** A descending spider goes head first on its safety line (popular accounts; a filmed
  spider lowering an egg sac faced downward). The line comes from the spinnerets at the back of the abdomen, so it
  hangs spinnerets up.
- **Speed is braked inside the body.** Spiders control how fast they lower themselves by regulating the force needed to
  pull new silk; an internal friction brake in the silk gland is suggested for *Araneus diadematus*
  (Gosline, "Scaling and Function of Spider Safety Line"). In some species one leg IV holds the line during a
  descent, in others no leg touches it (Journal of Arachnology 43(3):417).
- **Stop and climb back.** Spiders make controlled descents by spinning more silk, or return up the line, gathering it
  into a bundle as they climb; they often turn and climb part way back up the dragline (J. Arachnol. 43(3):417).

How v2.0124 uses it: drops are head first with legs spread and gently flexed (no stiff star); the brake is internal (a
leg holding the line cut through the abdomen, so no leg holds it); climbs back use the front two pairs hand over hand
(gathering), back pairs folded; at the top the body bends back so its belly comes up to the roof and the front legs
grab it first.
